Purpose

This is NOT a lead list for outreach. This is the benchmark set -- the wholesalers who ALREADY sell online consistently. The purpose is to study what makes them work and extract playbooks that can be applied to the offline-majority wholesalers in Lists A, F, and I. There are two distinct groups: (1) 35 wholesalers that are 100% online -- likely eCommerce-native or small K2K-only operations, and (2) 8 wholesalers with a meaningful mix of online+offline -- these are the real models to study because they've solved the transition challenge.

Key Insights

Insight 1: The "100% online" group is a different species

35 wholesalers sell 100% online with 0% stddev (perfectly consistent). These are likely K2K eCommerce-only operations -- they don't have a walk-in cooler or phone sales. They're useful as proof of concept but NOT as models for traditional wholesalers who need to add online to an existing offline business.

Insight 2: The "mixed model" group is the real playbook

8 wholesalers operate a genuine dual-channel business. Mayesh (24% online, $94M total) is the ceiling. KBC (13.6%, $64M) and Bill Doran (3.5%, $61M) are the Big 3. Sims (12.4%), Maple Grove (30.2%), Zeidler (17.2%), Juliet (10.9%), and Metropetals (8.7%) round out the group. THESE are the models to study.

Insight 3: Consistency matters more than peak

Mayesh maintains 19.6-29.5% online ratio month-over-month (stddev 4.0). KBC is 10.8-16.6% (stddev 2.1). These aren't one-month spikes -- they're sustained operational models. Compare to Juliet (5-20%, stddev 5.5) which is volatile. Consistency = real adoption, not a promotional blip.

Enablement Kit

WHY This List Matters (Strategic Logic)

  • Playbook source: "82% of GMV is offline" is the problem. These wholesalers are the ones who've cracked part of it. Understanding HOW they did it = the playbook for everyone else.
  • H3 - Digitalize: The insight from the ecosystem analysis is that "offline" wholesalers already BUY digital (Procurement) -- the gap is sell-side. These always-on wholesalers have solved the sell-side. What's different about them?
  • Credibility in the field: When CS tells a wholesaler "you should list inventory online," the response is "nobody does that." Having a benchmark list of 43 wholesalers who DO it, with names and numbers, is the credibility ammunition.

Research Questions (for CS interviews)

  1. "What triggered your decision to sell online? Was it a specific event, a customer request, or a gradual shift?"
  2. "How do you manage inventory for online orders vs walk-in customers? Do you reserve stock, or is it first-come-first-served?"
  3. "What percentage of your online orders come from new customers vs existing customers who used to call?"
  4. "What's the most common product type your online buyers order? Is it the same as walk-in, or different?"
  5. "If you could change one thing about the online selling tools, what would it be?"

What We're Looking For

  • Operational model: How do they keep catalog fresh? Dedicated person? Time of day? Automation?
  • Customer acquisition: Did online ATTRACT new buyers or just shift existing ones from phone?
  • Product selection: Do they list everything or a curated subset? Which products sell best online?
  • Channel conflict: Did they face pushback from sales staff who felt threatened by online orders?
  • ROI story: Can they quantify the benefit? "We saved X hours/week" or "We gained Y new customers"

Who to Assign

  • Facu: Own the benchmark analysis. Select 3-4 mixed-model wholesalers for deep-dive CS interviews (suggested: Zeidler, Maple Grove, Sims, Metropetals).
  • CS (Cata): Schedule and execute the discovery interviews using the research questions above. 1 interview per week, record on Fathom.
  • Mercurio: Build playbook from interview findings. What's replicable? What's specific to that wholesaler's context?
