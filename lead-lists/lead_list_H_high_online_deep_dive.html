Enablement Kit

WHY This Deep Dive Matters (Strategic Logic)

  • H3 validated: These 6 wholesalers prove that mixed online/offline models work. The question is not "can wholesalers sell online?" but "what conditions make it happen?"
  • Three distinct models emerged: (1) Boxes-via-eCommerce (FloraLink, Sims, KBC-Boxes), (2) Units-via-eCommerce (Maple Grove, Zeidler), (3) Everything-via-API (Mayesh). Each requires a different playbook.
  • The #1 growth lever is Units-via-eCommerce. Boxes are already online for the leaders. The $49M+ gap is in loose bunches/stems. Maple Grove and Zeidler prove the model works. KBC is the biggest prize.

Key Questions for Deep-Dive Interviews

  1. "Walk me through your morning: how does cooler inventory get listed online? Who does it? How long does it take?"
  2. "Your online buyers -- are they mostly the same florists who used to call, or are they new customers who found you online?"
  3. "What products sell best online vs walk-in? Is there a difference in what they buy or how much?"

Expected Outcomes

  • Maple Grove interview: Document the "15-minute morning routine" for listing cooler inventory. Package as a how-to.
  • Zeidler interview: Understand the pure Units eShop model. What are the 14 categories? How minimal is the effort?
  • Sims interview: What triggered the $0-to-$176K ramp? Was it CS-driven, self-driven, or event-driven?

Expected Objections (when presenting findings to offline wholesalers)

1. "Mayesh is huge -- that model doesn't apply to me"
Response: That's why we studied Maple Grove ($668K total) and Zeidler ($951K total). They're your size. Maple Grove lists 111 categories of loose bunches online and 55 buyers order that way. Zeidler does it with just 14 categories and 35 buyers. The model scales down.
2. "They probably have more staff / better tech"
Response: Zeidler runs this with the same Komet platform you already use. No special tech. They list on eShop, buyers see it, buyers order. The effort is 15 minutes in the morning. We can show you exactly how they set it up.
3. "My customers don't shop online"
Response: 55 of Maple Grove's 79 buyers (70%) now order online. They didn't start that way -- they were all phone buyers first. The shift happened because the cooler inventory became visible. Florists browse on their phone at 6am before the shop opens.
