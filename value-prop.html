Komet Core (ERP)

Wholesaler Importer
"Run your whole operation in one floral ERP — buy, hold, sell, ship, bill — and plug straight into the Komet buy/sell network so you sell more of what you already move."

Key Value Claims

  • Floral-native, not generic — built for boxes/bunches/stems, land cost, COGS breakdowns, Days of Service. Not a horizontal ERP bolted onto flowers. PROVEN
  • One system, kills double entry — SOs, Prebooks, POs, Order Entry, invoicing and AR in one place. Auto-create PO from vendor eShop = "phenomenal, that would save a lot of time." PROVEN
  • The network is the moat — K2K lets you show and sell a supplier's inventory you don't own. "The magic isn't the ERP, it's how we help them buy and sell better." PROVEN
  • Cloud + margin control — per-customer price lists and advanced pricing tiers. Margin flexes by customer profile automatically. PROVEN

Objections

FAILED
"Komet is 5-10x more expensive" (Carbone: $60/mo to $3,000/mo, walked)
Don't lead with ARR; recoup via payments. Payments-offset untested. Carbone, FloraMax, Webb's all lost on price.
NO ANSWER
"You'll own my business" (independence fear)
No proven rebuttal. Real, often unrecoverable.
PROPOSED
"Implementations feel endless"
Set 60-90 day window upfront. Onsite accelerates to days. Name owners and timelines.
GAP
"We sell hardgoods and Komet makes us fake them as flowers"
Unified model now treats HG as first-class — but V1 is new companies only. Do not commit to existing accounts.
GAP
"QuickBooks Online not supported"
Product gap. QB Desktop only. Cash/check sync has gaps.

Proof Points

FCS migration prospect — evaluating Core as cloud replacement after FCS/Smart sunset. Auto-PO from vendor eShop rated "phenomenal." PROVEN
Flora Fresh — live on Core inventory, adopted Inventory Evaluation Report. PROVEN
FGS (Jeroen) — eSuite webshop "quite simplified," K2K connections "super, super helpful." PROVEN
JFS Wholesale — onsite go-live, ~$1.1M through Koronet Payments on a ~$2M wholesaler. PROVEN

What It Does NOT Do

  • Hardgoods unified model = new companies only. Existing customers on legacy 3-model until separate migration.
  • QuickBooks Desktop only. No QBO. Cash/check payments don't sync bidirectionally.
  • Single session per user seat — no simultaneous multi-session.
  • Route management = customer-to-route association only, no optimization.
  • eShop filters (color, length, grade) incomplete with rapid-click UX bug.

Koronet for Wholesalers

Pitch by segment — 271 wholesalers, what to say to each

The strategic reframe

The value prop is digitalize the existing book, not acquire net-new. The gap is online penetration of existing buyers, not buyer scarcity. Low online % on a big book = high absolute headroom, not low adoption.

Core Only

245 of 271 wholesalers (90%) | The majority. 44 sell bunches offline at <10% online but lack platform to go digital.
If you have Core but don't have eShop/eSuite → Your florists can't see what you have. You're losing orders to whoever shows up online first. Your opportunity is to put your floor inventory online — starting with bunches.

What to pitch

  • Procurement — buy from all your vendors in one cart. Start here, no selling required.
  • eShop/eSuite upgrade — to sell bunches online. Core alone cannot do this.
  • The sequence: Buy Better (Procurement) → realize you need to sell online → add eShop/eSuite → publish bunches → price parity → grow.

Specific Objections

GAP
"I'm Core-only — I CAN'T sell bunches online even if I wanted to" — Fact. 245 of 271 wholesalers. They need eShop/eSuite. This is a platform capability question.
PROPOSED
"eShops upsell is $600/mo — I can't justify that" — If 5% of your offline book shifts online, the fees pay for the upsell many times over. Unproven ROI argument.

Best in Class

KBC Pittsburgh — 453 buyers, 383 active, only 129 ever online. Units catalog not uploaded (0.05% vs peers 5-12%). The store has the boxes but not the floor. Shows the headroom. DATA-PROVEN

Core + Procurement (buys digital, sells analog)

The "offline is a misnomer" pattern — uses digital tools to BUY but sales are phone/walk-in
If you have Procurement but don't sell online → You already buy digitally. Your opportunity is to publish what you buy and let your florists order online too. Core+Proc wholesalers grew eCom +23%. Core-only went -44%.

What to pitch

  • Validate they're getting value from Procurement first — then pivot to "now sell what you buy."
  • eShop/eSuite — publish your procured inventory. Buyers see it, order it.
  • Procurement + eCommerce are COMPLEMENTS. Enabling buying helps selling.

Specific Objections

PROPOSED
"Online means a 1.5% fee on volume that was free offline" — ROI argument: efficiency, reach, incremental volume outweigh fee. Free-fee onboarding buys time. #1 challenge.

Best in Class

DKAY — 62 connections, $469K procurement, $0 selling. Buys digitally, sells analog. DATA-PROVEN
Arizona Family — 113 connections, $2.3M procurement, $0 selling. Same pattern. DATA-PROVEN

Core + eShop (has store, underusing it)

Has the storefront but it's a ghost catalog. Likely stuck between Stage 2 and Stage 3.
If you have an eShop but less than 10% online → Your store exists but doesn't convert. Your opportunity is to fix what's broken: upload bunches, match floor pricing, train reps.

Checklist — make sure you use these well

  • Are bunches uploaded? (Most: no)
  • Does online pricing match offline? (Most: no)
  • Are reps using the eShop or steering orders away? (Most: steering away)
  • Is the eShop an actual sales channel or a ghost catalog? (Most: ghost)
  • Are deals and terms configured online? (Most: not fully)

Specific Objections

PROPOSED
"My reps won't use it — they steer orders offline to avoid the fee" — Rep enablement play: "Your reps sell UP instead of typing orders." The eShop handles reorders. Siedlers adopted this framing.
NO ANSWER
"Bunch upload is too much work — I can't photograph every bunch every morning" — Core adoption blocker. The tool exists but nobody uses it. Needs operational support, not a rebuttal.

Siedlers — 12-13% to 19% online in 90 days by matching online to offline + not cannibalizing reps. REPORTED

TX Initiative Talking Points

6 canonical initiatives — the operating focus of Chapter TX

The unified model

Grow GMV → Digitalize GMV → Monetize digital GMV into durable fees.
Digitalization mechanism: BUY digitally → LIST bought/sellable supply online → SELL through digital sales motion.

1. Digital Enablers — Build → Adopt → Learn

Convert product capability into commercial behavior. Product delivery does not equal revenue impact. Once an enabler is delivered, the work shifts to adoption and learning. When an enabler is in MVP/testing, the work is to validate real usage. When it's WIP, the work is to define expected behavior and launch KPI before Revenue over-promises it.
The Number
3 status lanes: Delivered / Testing / WIP
Every enabler must be labeled. Delivered: buyer onboarding, self-service checkout, all-in pricing. Testing: Axerrio K2K beta, Supplies HG. WIP: eShop/eSuite parity unlocks.

Talking Points

  • Shipped =/= used. We have enablers sitting in accounts that nobody knows about or nobody adopted. The loop is: build, drive adoption, capture why it's not used, fix repeating blockers.
  • Don't over-promise WIP. If the enabler is still being built, define the expected commercial behavior and launch KPI BEFORE Revenue sells it. The loss pattern (Carbone, FloraMax) came from pitching unshipped capabilities.
  • Blockers split into 6 categories: training, sales motion, inventory, pricing/terms, delivery, or product gap. Most "product gaps" turn out to be training or sales motion issues.
  • Buyer onboarding (self-register) and self-service checkout are delivered and in adoption mode. Track # accounts using them, # buyers/orders through them.

Expected Objections

PROPOSED
"The product isn't ready" — Some things are. Some aren't. This initiative forces clarity on which is which. Don't let "product isn't ready" become the excuse for not driving adoption of what IS ready.

Owner

Product build: Agus/Fede (Maga coordinates). GTM adoption: Revenue, CS, Implementation. Data: Rose. Enablement: Socrates.

2. Connection Funnel — Digitalize GMV: BUY

Help wholesalers buy better by activating the right connected supply and turning connections into repeat procurement. Better buying creates the input for LIST. If wholesalers do not repeatedly buy useful supply, there is nothing meaningful to list or sell online.
The Number
~48% of K2K pairs silent 6+ months
The leak is retention, not "never online." 20,949 established buyer-seller pairs have gone silent for 3+ months. That's the real win-back list.

Talking Points

  • Buyer-requested connections (Easy Handshake) convert at 57% vs blitz/ops-created at ~13%. The highest-quality connections are the ones the client asks for.
  • Connection churn is the problem, not "never online." ~48% of K2K pairs are silent for 6+ months. The pairs were online and stopped.
  • Follow the buyer. The buyer's motivation determines whether a connection converts. Contact from buyer side AND vendor side.
  • Initiative sources matter: Easy Handshake, Cupido, Daza, Blitz, manual/handshake — each has different conversion and learning.
  • K2K grew +41% YoY. The network is expanding. The question is whether activated pairs STAY active.

Expected Objections

NO ANSWER
"Why did they stop buying?" — Could be awareness, supply, price, logistics, terms, or sales motion. This initiative's job is to find out. No single answer.

Owner

Business: Cata. Product/data: Fede/Agus. Data: Rose. Enablement: Socrates. Follow-up tracking: Pablito.

3. Strategic Account Plays

Turn priority accounts into explicit plays with owner, ask, action, metric, and learning — instead of generic "top account" attention. Large accounts, mid-tier, and small role models need different motions. The system learns fastest when each play is explicit and measured.
The Number
# accounts with an approved play
The leading indicator. If accounts don't have explicit plays, they're getting generic attention.

Talking Points

  • Three tracks: Whales (custom plays, exec alignment, high leverage), Mid-tier (Black Belt / structured program), Small role models (learn what works, replicate).
  • Every play must have: what we believe, what we ask, what we do, what we measure, what we learn next.
  • Validated plays become reusable and go into the plays registry. Rejected plays log why — learning either way.
  • Onsite is the proven win lever. "$2,500, pays off month 1 in payment fees; we win in person, not on Zoom."

Expected Objections

PROPOSED
"We don't have bandwidth for custom plays on every account" — Start with 5. If the plays work, the motions become templates. If they don't, we learn why without scaling failure.

Owner

Business: Cata + Facu. Account strategy: Mercurio. Data: Rose. Enablement: Socrates. Deadlines: Pablito. Verification: Codex.

4. Online Supply Readiness — Digitalize GMV: LIST

Make the supply wholesalers actually buy and sell offline visible, priced, reliable, and sellable online. Digital demand cannot convert against empty shelves, incomplete catalogs, stale availability, missing units, unclear terms, or inventory workflows teams cannot maintain.
The Number
$2.56B bunch GMV, 0.4% online
The biggest supply gap in the ecosystem. 48 of 51 bunch-sellers have less than 10% online. K2K cannot sell bunches at all ($0).

Talking Points

  • What the wholesaler sells on the floor is NOT what appears online. The eShop shows boxes. The cooler has bunches. To a florist, the eShop looks like a different business.
  • Only ~22-29 wholesalers CAN sell bunches online. 245 of 271 are Core-only. The binding constraint is channel capability, not willingness.
  • The bunch upload tool exists. Adoption is approximately zero. "Is anyone adding bunches? Crickets." This is an operational problem, not a product problem.
  • Zeidler proves it works. Same implementation effort as Riverside (0.09% digital). Zeidler is at 17.2%. The difference is operational commitment + bunch listing.
  • Supplies/Hardgoods is a separate track: $14.1M GMV (83% Bill Doran). Gated by imagery, taxonomy, case-selling parity.

Expected Objections

NO ANSWER
"Bunch upload is too much work every morning" — The core adoption blocker. No proven answer. Needs operational support model, not a rebuttal.

Owner

Business: Cata. Supporting: CS, Implementation, Product. Data: Rose. Enablement: Socrates.

5. Commercial Readiness in Implementation — Digitalize GMV: SELL

Ensure implementation leaves wholesalers ready to transact, not merely configured. Listing supply is not enough. The client needs buyer access, pricing/terms clarity, sales-team alignment, delivery setup, and a first commercial motion to convert the existing book.
The Number
Signed =/= Activated
0 transactions 60+ days post-launch is the failure signal. Measure >=1 real transaction, not "it's enabled."

Talking Points

  • 6 readiness dimensions: supply readiness, price/deal/terms, buyer access, delivery/pickup/payment, sales rep motion, first commercial play.
  • The System Health Check — prefilled for each account before go-live. Client-friendly explanation of what's missing.
  • "Signed =/= activated" is the operating principle. We measure first order, repeat orders, and online GMV from implemented accounts — not signatures.
  • The implementation window is 60-90 days. After that, fatigue sets in. "Implementations feel endless" is a real churn signal.
  • Implementation learnings route back to Product/GTM. If the same gap appears 3x, it's not a client problem — it's a system problem.

Expected Objections

PROPOSED
"Implementation is a service team function, not a revenue function" — If the implementation doesn't produce a first transaction, it's a cost with no return. Readiness IS revenue.

Owner

Business: Christine (reports through Cata). Enablement: Socrates. Data: Rose. Plays: Mercurio.

6. Growth / Black Belt — Grow + Digitalize

Use Black Belt to amplify SELL when it works and diagnose when it does not. Growth is not only ads. Black Belt amplifies demand, increases active online behavior, and understands where the funnel breaks through readiness, CRM, Deal Maker, eShop tuning, retargeting, traffic, and measurement.
The Number
Online >= Offline (the readiness standard)
Online must be at least as good as offline across availability, pricing, deals, terms, delivery/pickup, relationship, and sales-team trust. If any dimension is worse online, digitalization stalls.

Talking Points

  • Growth is gated on readiness. Don't bring traffic to an empty store with wrong prices. Stages 1-3 come before Stage 4.
  • The addressable market for most wholesalers is 200-500 florists. It's limited. Acquisition support is exclusive — only for those who earned it by doing Stages 1-3.
  • Black Belt operating modes: Readiness check, existing-client digitalization, eShop tuning, CRM/Deal Maker, traffic/acquisition, diagnostic mode.
  • Measure the funnel: invite → click → register → eShop visit → first order → repeat. CPL/CPA by stage, not aggregate leads.
  • When the blocker is monetization/product/business-model (Standing Orders, Grower Portal, API/EDI, billing leakage) — route to leadership. Don't pretend the Friday operating group can solve it.

Expected Objections

PROPOSED
"My customers are old, they won't switch online" — Still 85% of your book. We start by digitalizing the buyers you already have. Siedlers went 12% to 19%. But this hasn't closed a skeptic in the room.
NO ANSWER
"What does Black Belt actually cost and deliver?" — No Black Belt offer exists: what client gets, what client does, what metric proves it, what happens if it fails. GAP.
