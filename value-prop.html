Coaching Talk Tracks

Give Feedback
The spine for everyone: help the wholesaler digitalize their existing book. Don't promise traffic, don't overclaim. Lead with the client's outcome; products are how, never the message.

SALES The Pitch + Objection Handling

Open (digitalize-led)
"You already do the volume -- mostly rep-keyed and offline. Our job is to put that book online: your buyers self-serve, your bunches sell online, and you reactivate the buyers who went quiet. Not new buyers -- the ones you have, ordering online."
Reason to believe

The network ($1.7B/yr, your buyers & suppliers already here) + "we win when you win."

Objection handling
  • "5-10x more expensive" -- don't lead with ARR; "we make our money other ways -- discount the SaaS, recoup on payments as card volume grows." PROPOSED
  • "Is Payments mandatory?" -- don't lead with the rate; lead product + onsite; rate negotiated later with data. PROPOSED
  • "You're too big -- you'll own my business." -- No proven rebuttal. Surface honestly, don't bluff. NO ANSWER
  • "My customers are old, won't switch online." -- "it's still 85% of your book for someone leaning in -- start by digitalizing the buyers you already have." PROPOSED
  • "K2K 1.5% feels like double-dipping." -- waive on Procurement / volume-waiver / phased. PROPOSED
  • Trust -- "we do what we tell and tell what we do." PROPOSED

CS Expansion Plays (by segment)

  • Big-book / low-online (H&T, Flora Fresh): get bunches uploaded (tool exists, adoption near 0); push online/offline parity; drive existing buyers online (CRM).
  • Core-bunch (44): enable eShop/eSuite, upload bunches, digitalize.
  • Silent buyers (~21K): run the ranked reactivation list -- CRM win-back; follow the buyer to repeat; flag standing-order connections for credit.
  • K2K box-sellers (98): add the storefront to sell bunches (growth ceiling on box-only).
  • Supplies-heavy: the supplies-online attach (gated by parity).
North star: online order share up, reactivated buyers, payments penetration.

IMPL Set This Live First

  • Parity first: upload bunches and match floor pricing/availability -- before any marketing (else reps steer off-platform, buyers keep calling).
  • Activation, not feature-flag-ON: success metric = 1+ real transaction without reverting to workarounds, not "it's enabled."
  • Onsite go-live (enterprise): "we win in person, not on Zoom" -- pays for itself in payment fees month 1.
  • Supplies: remember the eShop publish step (supplies don't surface automatically on migration).
  • Payments friction: eSuite is card-only today -- set the contract disclaimer to enable Koronet Payments when ACH ships.
The rule for all three roles: Never promise traffic. Never claim a capability that isn't live for their product (Core vs eShops-upgrade vs eSuite differ).

US Battlecards

Give Feedback
Reframe: FCS/SMART is ours (Koronet acquired it Dec 2023) -- not a battlecard, a migration playbook. The real external competitor = DV Flora (+ where holdouts flee: WooCommerce/self-built).

DV Flora (Delaware Valley Floral Group)

Vertically-integrated wholesaler + software + logistics (1959, NJ). Dutch Direct e-commerce + native iOS/Android app since ~2014. Acquisitive -- rolls up regional wholesalers (recently acquired Rokay, an FCS/Koronet account).

Where Koronet WINS

  • Neutral operating system for a wholesaler's OWN business -- DV Flora's app only sells DV Flora's product
  • Network supply (K2K + Procurement, farms/importers) -- a closed single-supplier storefront can't offer that
  • Payments monetization (ACH $1, cards 2.9%+$0.25, auto-charge on terms) -- a value layer they don't sell

Where THEY Win (be honest)

  • For a florist who just wants flowers next-day in a clean app, DV Flora is a complete bundle (flowers + trucks + app) -- we own neither flowers nor logistics
  • Roll-ups physically remove accounts (acquire a wholesaler, migrate onto DV's stack)
  • Hardgoods in one storefront today -- we're still closing that gap (R1-R3, ~Aug 2026)
Lead angle: "your own business on a neutral platform + the whole network's supply" vs "a storefront that only sells one wholesaler's flowers."

FCS / SMART -- Migration Playbook

On-prem .NET/C#/SQL ERP. SMART reports = the standout clients fear losing. SMARTSite = basic ecom. Desktop/on-prem -- can't be updated, scaled, or shut down remotely. Security/continuity risk ("could fail any day"). ~19 FCS accounts (~$846K new-ARR pipeline).

Why They Move (the pitch)

  • Cloud + real e-commerce + payments + procurement network -- everything FCS structurally can't do
  • Frame as risk-removal, not "better features"
  • Land-and-expand on FEES, not SaaS (up to 80% SaaS discount yr1; monetize payments + procurement tx-fees; 98% retention once in ecosystem)

Where We Lose

  • Price-sensitive holdouts reject the jump, ride Extended Access indefinitely
  • Flee to WooCommerce / self-built / cheaper stacks
  • Get acquired by DV Flora
  • "I'll lose my SMART reports" -- replicate SMART reports in Komet (open action)

Proven Conversion Levers

  • On-site go-live + porting saved payment methods: JFS got 77% of sales on Koronet Payments in 3 days; Flora Fresh 40%
  • Price's closed in <2 weeks once "no isn't really an option"
  • Resolve Extended Access billing disputes before pitching
Net (US): Win DV Flora on neutral-OS + network. Win FCS holdouts on risk-removal + fees-not-SaaS + onsite/payment-porting + SMART-report parity. Shared threat to neutralize: price-sensitive flight -- the fee-led commercial model is the answer.
