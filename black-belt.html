TX Growth / Black Belt

Help wholesalers grow repeat online transactions.

Black Belt is the Growth program. It helps a wholesaler understand who buys, what they buy, when they buy, why they still call or text, and what would make online selling realistic enough to repeat and scale.

Core definition

Black Belt is not “more marketing.” It is a tested path to repeat online behavior.

Customer job

Sell more online with less wasted effort.

The wholesaler should feel this as expert support: better cohorts, better timing, better supply presentation, better follow-up, and clearer measurement.

Koronet job

Learn what it takes to digitalize the sales motion.

Some tests will scale. Some will expose readiness, product, pricing, incentive, or workflow blockers. Both outcomes are useful if measured.

Standard

Online must become good enough to win or reveal the break.

Availability, pricing, terms, delivery, relationship, and sales-team incentives must be strong enough that online can compete with calls/texts.

Operating modes

Diagnose, refine, amplify.

Diagnose

Find where the sales motion breaks.

Understand buyer cohorts, competitors, TAM, supply, pricing, timing, leakage, and readiness.

  • Who should buy online?
  • What are they trying to buy?
  • Why do they call, text, or negotiate offline?
  • What would make online easier or faster?
Refine

Test the motion before scaling spend.

Use expert-backed plays with a specific cohort, channel, message, offer, supply set, and timing.

  • Existing online buyers with low repeat.
  • Registered buyers with no purchase.
  • Offline buyers for categories already online.
  • Comparable accounts showing a better motion.
Amplify

Scale only when the base is ready enough.

Increase channel investment after repeat behavior, supply readiness, and tracking are strong enough.

  • Retained online buyer cohort.
  • Known channel or campaign works.
  • Supply can support demand.
  • Measurement can prove the effect.

What has to be true

Best candidates combine five signals.

SignalWhat it meansWhy it matters
Willingness / championSomeone at the account wants help and will act.Growth fails if the customer will not change the motion.
Supply qualityOnline supply is credible: format, variety, time depth, freshness, and fulfillment confidence.You cannot sell what is not listed well enough online.
Online buyer signalThere are active or potential buyers to refine or reactivate.Black Belt should not start from a blank buyer base unless it is explicitly diagnostic.
Offline-to-online upsideThere is meaningful behavior still happening by call, text, rep, or offline order.The upside is digitalizing real existing behavior, not just sending traffic.
MeasurabilityWe can track invite, click, visit, first order, repeat order, and leakage signals.If we cannot measure it, we cannot learn or scale it responsibly.

What belongs in Black Belt

Levers should be tied to a blocker and a measurement plan.

Examples of valid levers

  • Customer cohort strategy.
  • CRM / segmented broadcasts.
  • Deep links, tags, featured deals, hot deals.
  • Retargeting and measured traffic.
  • eShop tuning based on buyer behavior.
  • Sales-team incentives that keep transactions online.
  • Leakage discovery: calls, texts, price negotiation, manual sourcing.

Boundary

  • Do not scale traffic into weak supply unless the goal is diagnostic.
  • Do not treat multi-location strategic accounts as the default core list.
  • Do not assume API sellers are eShop operating models; use them for learning.
  • Route product blockers to Digital Enablers only after the blocker is proven.
  • Route simple readiness gaps back to BUY, LIST, or SELL.

Lautaro angle

How this should help messaging and value prop work.

The Black Belt message should not be “Koronet will run marketing.” It should be: we help you understand which buyers should buy online, what they need, what blocks them, and which measured plays can make online selling repeatable.

The strongest value prop is customer-specific: current buyers, current supply, competitor gaps, timing, and the sales routines that can be digitized without pretending the industry will change overnight.
