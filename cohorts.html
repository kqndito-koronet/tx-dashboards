Wholesaler Cohorts: Who Sells Online and Why

143 wholesalers ranked by online %, grouped into 4 cohorts. The question: what separates those who sell online from those who don't? Answer: catalog coverage and inventory format (Boxes vs Units), not company size.
82 of 143 wholesalers (Cohorts C+D) have near-zero online sales. But the 8 in Cohort B prove it works at scale.
  • Cohort A (>20% online): 32 wholesalers, but mostly born-digital, all Boxes, small. NOT role models for big wholesalers.
  • Cohort B (5-20%): KBC, Mayesh, BDC, Zeidler, Sims. THE action cohort. They prove large wholesalers can go digital.
  • The predictor is catalog coverage: Mayesh lists 63% of products online = 24% online GMV. BDC lists 16% = 3.5%. Put products online, buyers come.
  • Boxes vs Units = 6x gap: Same product (Rose), Boxes 29.3% online vs Units 4.5%. Format, not flower, drives digital adoption.
  • Dual-channel buyers are 3.7x more valuable ($20,754 vs $5,561). Online does not cannibalize — it compounds.

Why Boxes go online and Units don't
Boxes = standard count, pre-orderable, no inspection needed. Units = variable stems, walk-in selection, "I want to see it." The product format itself determines digital readiness. Zeidler and Maple Grove are the rare Units-digital exceptions (role models).

Surprising Findings

Counter-intuitive patterns from the cohort analysis
4.3 days
Online orders placed further ahead
Online avg lead time 4.3 days vs 2.9 days offline. Online buyers plan ahead, not impulse-buy. This means online captures different demand, not the same demand faster.
76-83%
Online is prebooks, not spot market
76-83% of online orders are prebooks (advance orders). Online is a planning channel, not a spontaneous one. This contradicts the "eCommerce = instant" assumption.
3.7x
Dual-channel buyers more valuable
Buyers who use both online + offline spend $20,754 avg vs $5,561 for single-channel. Online doesn't cannibalize offline — it compounds total spend.
88.8%
First-time online buyers return
Nearly 9 in 10 buyers who try online come back. The activation barrier is the hard part — retention is almost automatic once they start.
Holidays
Holidays HURT online %
During peak holiday periods, buyers revert to offline. When stakes are high (Valentine's, Mother's Day), trust shifts back to phone/in-person. Online % drops during the weeks florists need reliability most.
Units
The format gap is structural, not behavioral
Units (loose stems) are 7.3% online vs Boxes at 24.1%. Same buyers, same wholesalers. The product format itself — not the seller or buyer — determines whether it goes digital.
