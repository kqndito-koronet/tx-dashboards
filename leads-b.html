WHY These Accounts (Strategic Logic)

These 238 pairs are the most direct evidence: someone wired the connection, but nobody turned on the tap. The buyer is ALREADY purchasing from this vendor -- offline.   • Lowest friction activation: No new connection needed, no new vendor onboarding, no buyer acquisition. The setup work is done. This is a "flip the switch" opportunity.
  • Fee impact: Every dollar that moves from offline ($0 fee) to K2K online generates double-charged fees (sell-side + buy-side). The top 25 pairs alone represent $13.8M in offline GMV.
  • Connection Funnel health: Understanding WHY these wired connections never transacted is the #1 diagnostic insight for the entire Connection Funnel initiative (P6).

What to Ask (Discovery Script -- 5 questions)

  1. "We see you have a K2K connection with [vendor] set up. Do you know it's there? Have you ever tried placing an order through Komet from them?"
  2. "You're buying $X/month from [vendor] by phone/fax. What's your current ordering workflow -- who places the order, how often, and what triggers it?"
  3. "If you could see [vendor]'s available inventory in Komet and order directly, would that save time in your current process?"
  4. "What would need to be true for you to place your next [vendor] order through Komet instead of by phone? Is it a product visibility issue, a pricing issue, or a habit issue?"
  5. "If we helped you place your first order through the K2K connection this week, would you be willing to try it? What product would you start with?"

Expected Result Per Call

  • Best case: First K2K order placed during the call (pilot product identified, order submitted)
  • Good case: Buyer agrees to try K2K for their next weekly order; follow-up scheduled within 7 days
  • Discovery case: Learn the specific blocker (awareness? catalog gap? pricing? UX?) and classify it
  • Metric: 10% activation in 30 days = 24 pairs placing first K2K orders

Expected Objections

1. "I didn't know that connection existed / I don't know what K2K is"
Response: Most connections were set up via Blitz (mass connection). This means your vendor opted in, but nobody showed you how to use it. Let me walk you through it -- you'll be able to see their available inventory and order in 2 clicks. The buyer never asked for this; they need awareness first, not a pitch.
2. "I already order from them by phone -- it works fine, why change?"
Response: Phone works, but you can only order what's on your usual list. K2K lets you browse everything they have -- you might find a variety or deal you'd miss on the phone. Plus, your order history is logged automatically. We're not replacing the phone; we're giving you a window into their full inventory.
3. "Their catalog online is empty / doesn't have what I need"
Response: That's exactly the feedback we need. We'll flag this to the vendor -- if their buyers can't see inventory online, they're losing potential orders. Can you tell me the top 3 products you buy from them? We'll check if they're listed and escalate if not. (This feeds back into H2 -- supply gap.)

Key Patterns in This List

1. Continental Floral dominance: 6 of top 50 pairs involve Continental Floral LLC as vendor. They have many Blitz connections that never activated. Suggests a systemic vendor-side issue -- Continental may not have their catalog online, or their buyers don't know the K2K connection exists.

2. Blitz = awareness gap: ~85% of untransacted connections came from Blitz (mass connection). The buyer likely never asked for the connection and may not know it exists. Contrast with Handshake connections, which were explicitly requested and still didn't transact -- those suggest a different blocker (catalog/pricing/UX).

3. Importer-to-Wholesaler corridor: The vast majority of these pairs are importers selling to wholesalers (Importer vendor, Wholesaler buyer). This is the K2K corridor that's already working well at scale -- these are just the pairs that didn't activate within it.

4. KBC anomaly: Kennicott (#1 at $9.6M) is a wholesaler-to-florist pair (Ashland Addison). This is the H2/H3 corridor gap -- different play than importer-to-wholesaler. Requires eShop/bunch listing, not just K2K activation.
