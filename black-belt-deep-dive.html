  • Direct farm access for unique varieties

  • Volume advantages through K2K network

  • Help maintain supply online: not just buy better but ensure what they buy STAYS available digitally

Weekly Variance: What It Means and What We Do

Weekly variance = how much your online product count changes week to week. If you have 200 products Monday and 90 Friday, your customers learn the eShop is unreliable and stop checking.

What we do about it:

  • Track your product count every week — automated monitoring, no guessing
  • Alert you when it drops below your baseline — before customers notice
  • Diagnose the cause: upload schedule? Staff availability? System issue? Vendor delivery gap?
  • Set a minimum product count target with you — agreed-upon floor that never breaks
  • Benchmark: BDC runs 3% variance (rock solid). Zeidler ran 55% (trust problem). Target: <15%.

Full Catalog Online

  • Everything they sell offline should be visible online — bunches, boxes, open market, hard goods if applicable
  • Price parity: online prices must match or beat offline and competitor pricing
  • Photos on high-movers — Mayesh benchmark: 95%+ have images
  • Catalog coverage >80% of offline products as target

What makes this hard (and what we're learning)

Bunches require daily upload — boxes flow from procurement, bunches need manual or scanner input.

It's easier to sell boxes — pre-ordered, one format, automated. Bunches are the 93% gap.

The leakage is easy — a florist texts "got any roses?" and the rep says "yeah come grab them." No eShop needed. We compete against free, frictionless offline.

Photos take effort — Mayesh has 95%+ photos. Most wholesalers have less than 20%. We don't pretend this is easy. We help you close these gaps one at a time, measuring what works.

Leading indicators Products online / offline ratio; weekly product count variance <15%; % products available >7 days

Grow Your Customers — Cohort by Cohort

Five Customer Cohorts, Five Playbooks

C1

Active Digital Buyers

Already buying online. Your best customers in the digital channel.

GOAL: More frequency, larger baskets, better experience — make the digital channel irresistible.
What We Do
  • Reorder prediction: notify them when their usual products are available — before they have to search for them
  • Cross-sell engine: "customers who bought X also bought Y" — data-driven product recommendations
  • Specials and promotions: featured products, time-limited offers on slow-movers or new arrivals
  • Ease of use: save favorites, 1-click reorder, order templates for weekly staples
Channels and Tactics
  • Customer.io personalized email: "Your usual roses are in stock this week" with direct link to cart
  • eShop push notifications: availability alerts on favorited products
  • SMS for time-sensitive: flash sales, limited-quantity specials, holiday pre-orders
  • In-platform: personalized homepage, "for you" section based on purchase history
Frequency (orders/month) AOV ($ per order) Basket size (items per order) Retention rate (90d) Reorder rate
Example in Practice

Zeidler has 22 active online buyers. We identify the top 10 by frequency. Sarah at Main Street Flowers orders roses every Tuesday and lilies every Friday. On Monday at 6am, she gets an email: "Your Ecuadorian Red Roses are in stock — 24 stems, $32/bunch. Re-order now." She clicks, adds her usual lilies (cross-sell: "your Friday lilies are also ready"), and checks out in 90 seconds. Her AOV goes from $64 to $98. Multiply by 22 buyers. That is the engine.

C2

Dormant Digital

Were buying online, then stopped. The critical question: WHY?

GOAL: Understand why they stopped. Reactivate the valuable ones with a reason to come back.
What We Do
  • Identify: who stopped, when, what they used to buy, how much they were worth
  • Diagnose: was it a catalog gap? Price? Delivery issue? Bad experience? Or did they just forget?
  • Reactivate: personalized "we miss you" with their specific favorite products + a reason to come back (new arrivals, improved pricing, new features)
  • Win-back offer: if appropriate — time-limited, specific to what they bought (not a generic discount)
Channels and Tactics
  • Email campaign (Customer.io): 3-touch sequence — "We noticed you have not ordered in X weeks" → "Here is what is new" → "Come back with [offer]"
  • Sales rep follow-up call: personal touch for high-value dormants ($5K+/month historical)
  • Root cause survey: short, specific — "What would make you order online again?"
  • Fix-then-reach: if the diagnosis is catalog or price, fix it FIRST, then reach out
Reactivation rate (%) Time to reactivation (days) Post-reactivation retention (60d) Root cause distribution
Example in Practice

Zeidler has 20+ dormant digital accounts worth $99K+ in offline GMV. We pull the list by name. "Petal Perfect" last ordered online 4 months ago — $2,400/month historically. The diagnosis: they stopped when lily prices went up 18% online vs. phone pricing. We fix the price parity. Then: "Hi Maria, we noticed we had not seen you online in a while. Your Oriental Lilies are back at $28/bunch — same as phone pricing. Plus 6 new varieties this week." Maria logs in, sees her favorites are saved, reorders. $2,400/month recovered. Repeat 19 more times.

C3

Dormant Accounts

Have an account, never logged in. Many are active OFFLINE buyers — this is gold.

GOAL: Get them to try online for the first time. Show them THEIR products, not a generic catalog.
What We Do
  • Identify the gold: which dormant accounts are active OFFLINE buyers? Cross-reference account list with offline sales data — rank by revenue
  • Personalized outreach: "Hey, you already buy from us every week. Did you know you can see our full inventory online and reorder in 30 seconds?"
  • Guided walkthrough: show them THEIR usual products on the eShop — not a generic demo, their actual items
  • First-order incentive: free delivery on first online order, or rep-assisted first order (the rep enters it WITH them)
Channels and Tactics
  • Sales rep personal call: "I set up your account — your usual order is already saved. Want me to walk you through it?"
  • Email follow-up: account login link + "here is what is available for you this week"
  • Customer.io onboarding sequence: Day 1 welcome → Day 3 "here is how to search" → Day 7 "your products this week"
  • Rep handoff to digital: rep places the first order WITH the customer, then transitions to self-service

Example in Practice

Account "Blooms by Design" registered 6 months ago, never logged in, but buys $4,800/month offline. C4

Offline Buyers

Buy by phone or walk-in. No account. Often the highest-value customers.

GOAL: Create account, show the value of digital, convert to at least hybrid (orders online + phone).
What We Do
  • Rep enablement: the rep enters their next order THROUGH the platform — the customer does not change behavior, but the system captures the order
  • Account creation on their behalf: customer sees order confirmation email + "you now have an account"
  • Show order history: "Here is everything you have bought this quarter. Here is what is available this week."
  • Gradual migration: rep uses the platform as CRM. Customer gets used to seeing digital inventory and order confirmations. The transition happens naturally.
Channels and Tactics
  • Sales rep (primary): every phone order goes through the platform. The rep IS the channel.
  • Platform-generated emails (secondary): order confirmations, "what is new this week", specials
  • Hybrid nudge: after 3+ rep-entered orders: "Next time, you can reorder in 30 seconds from your phone"
  • The convenience pitch: not "go digital" but "see your order history, track delivery, reorder faster"

Example in Practice

Wholesaler has 120 offline-only buyers doing $3.2M/year by phone and walk-in. The rep starts entering orders through the platform for the top 30 accounts. Each customer gets an order confirmation email: "Here is your order from today. Your account is set up. See what else is available this week." After 3 orders, the system sends: "Next time, you can reorder your usual stems in 30 seconds from your phone." Target: 15% of the top 30 place at least one self-service order within 60 days. That is 4-5 buyers doing $400K+ who now show up in the digital funnel. From invisible to visible.

Fee Approach at Growth Partner Level

These are tests, not policy

Fee incentives are hypotheses to test, not committed discounts. We test at 1 account, measure whether the behavior changes, and scale only what works. Nothing is committed until validated.

  • Hypothesis: temporary fee relief while they make eShop their CRM could accelerate adoption dramatically
  • Test logic: $0 fees for 3 months IF they upload bunches, buy >10% through Procurement, update weekly best sellers
  • The math (illustrative): 3 months $0 on $5K/quarter = $5K cost. If 10% Procurement adoption on $2M offline = $30K new fee base. The behavior pays for the discount many times over.
  • Kill criteria: if catalog metrics and customer metrics do not improve within 60 days, the test ends
  • Test at 1 account first. JFS Wholesale is the candidate.

How We Operate

  • We triple down when supply + willingness exist — because that is when success probability is highest. Acquisition support is exclusive: only for the best.
  • Discovery before pitch, always. Every account gets a supply analysis and demand analysis before we propose plays. We show them their own data, not generic decks.
  • Fee incentives are earned through measurable behavior — not negotiated as a sales concession. The program is about growth, not cost reduction.
  • Always measuring, always learning. Every play has a hypothesis, a success metric, and kill criteria. We scale what works across accounts. We log what does not.
  • The sequence has dependencies but we study acceleration. If an account can move faster, we learn why and replicate.
  • On whales, we DO run growth plays — but always measuring. Where do we lose? Optimize. Where do we win? Double down.

6. Weekly Operating Model: The Friday Cadence

The Black Belt Program runs every week. Each initiative owner presents. We pre-fill slides with data. The loop closes or it dies.

When What Happens Who
Wednesday Pre-fill each initiative owner's slide with data: account metrics, funnel position, week-over-week changes. TX Chapter (data)
Thursday Initiative owners update their slide (24h before): what they did, what they learned, what changed. CS / Impl owners
Friday 1 mandatory slide per initiative. 5-7 min each. "Which accounts did you speak with? What did you learn?" Full chapter
Friday +1hr Capture learnings. Update account registry. Feed insights into play design. TX Chapter

Learning, not reporting

The Friday meeting is where knowledge compounds. We ask: what did you learn that you did not know before? Did it change the play? When they talk to a dormant florist, we want to know: did they know the eShop existed? Was price the barrier? Was it product availability? That learning feeds back into every cohort playbook.
