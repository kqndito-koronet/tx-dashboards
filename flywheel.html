How We Classified $428M of Unclassified GMV

4 approaches · 193 distinct customer_type values · 6,835+ buyer names matched

The Problem

SALE_DETAILS.customer_type is freetext. 193 distinct values including blank, tier codes (A/B/D/G), geography (USA/Europe/CIS), operational codes (Shipping/NB/XS), and hundreds of variants of the same concept ("Retailer"/"Retail"/"RETAILER"/"RET"/"Retail Florist"/"Retailer - Business"/etc).

ApproachWhat it classifiedGMV resolvedConfidence
1. customer_type normalization 193 distinct values mapped to 7 buyer types. "Retailer"/"Retail"/"RETAILER"/"RET" all → Retailer. "16. Supermarket WF" → Mass Market. "Bouquet Maker" → Bouquet Maker. etc. $580M HIGH
2. KBC tier-code decode A/B/D/G = KBC volume tiers (ALL from Kennicott). Name-matched grocery chains (Tony's, Caputo's, Sendik's) → Mass Market. Wholesaler names → Wholesaler. Remainder → Retailer (KBC's business is selling to florists). $63.0M HIGH
3. Rosaprima/Royal geo decode USA/Europe/CIS/ROW/Canada/Asia/Australia = Rosaprima + Royal Flowers destination-market codes. Name-matched known florists → Retailer. Remainder → Wholesaler (Rosaprima sells to importers/wholesalers, not end retailers). $56.6M MEDIUM-HIGH
4. Blank customer_type: name + seller default Matched customer_name ILIKE patterns for grocery/wholesale/florist/event/import keywords. Unmatched defaulted by seller type: Wholesaler sellers → Retailer buyers. Importer sellers → Wholesaler buyers. Grower sellers → Importer buyers. $148.3M MEDIUM
Unresolvable remainder Other/miscellaneous codes, unique operational types (eCom buyers, brokers, cooler rental, liquidation) $11.2M N/A (1.3%)

Key Discoveries

  • KBC tier codes (A/B/D/G) = $63M from one company. These are volume tiers, not buyer types. Tier A includes Tony's Finer Foods (grocery) AND Flowers4Dreams (florist) AND Kehoe Designs (event). Had to name-match each customer individually.
  • Rosaprima's $56.6M uses geography as customer_type. "USA" doesn't mean the buyer is in the US market layer — it means Rosaprima ships roses there. The buyers are overwhelmingly wholesalers (Delaware Valley, Kennicott, Len Busch) and importers.
  • "Internal" = $57M hiding in the ecosystem. Wholesaler inter-branch transfers ($48M) + importer intercompany (CFG, Fresca non-sale, $8.9M). This was inflating commercial GMV in the old table.
  • Wholesaler → Retailer nearly tripled ($73.8M → $213.9M) because KBC tier codes, Mayesh op codes (Shipping/NB/XS/MC/HC/NP), and blank customer_type all resolved to retailers.
  • The "default by seller type" assumption (Approach 4) accounts for ~$70M. This is the least certain bucket. A wholesaler's customer with blank type is PROBABLY a retailer, but could be another wholesaler or mass market. Conservative confidence: MEDIUM.

Why It Jams at Wholesaler → Retailer

Four structural barriers keeping $213.9M mostly offline

Structural Barriers

1
CATALOG: Products not listed online. Self-fulfilling prophecy confirmed. BDC has 22,102 products, only 16.4% online. If it's not listed, it can't be sold. This is the root cause.
2
STANDING ORDERS: $39.3M wholesaler SOs offline. No digital alternative. Retailers place recurring orders by phone/fax. No product exists to convert these to online. (Total SOs across all layers: $142.5M offline.)
3
TRUST: Holidays hurt online %. When stakes are high (Valentine's, Mother's Day), buyers revert to offline. They don't trust the platform for critical orders. Online % drops precisely when volume peaks.
4
CONCENTRATION: Remove Mayesh + KBC = thin digital base. The digital wholesaler base is thin. Two accounts drive the majority. If either stops, the layer collapses.
Order of attack: (1) Fix catalog coverage to unlock potential → (2) build digital SO product to capture recurring volume → (3) earn trust through holiday reliability → (4) expand beyond Mayesh/KBC to diversify.
