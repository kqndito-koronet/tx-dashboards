The flywheel spins at Importer → Wholesaler — and jams at Wholesaler → Retailer.

  • Concentration risk: Remove Mayesh + KBC and only $16.2M of digital volume remains across wholesalers.

BUY → LIST → SELL: The Wholesaler Conversion Funnel

The flywheel breaks at LIST
BUY
Wholesalers buy $17.7M digitally via Procurement (K2K).
KBC: $7.8M · BDC: $3.8M · Others: $6.1M
This is the input — product enters the wholesaler's system digitally.
↓
LIST
BDC has 22,102 products but only 16.4% listed online.
If it's not listed, it can't be sold online. This is where the funnel breaks.
Catalog coverage predicts adoption. Self-fulfilling prophecy: "nothing to buy online" because nothing is listed.
↓
SELL
Only $3.4M eCom in Wholesaler → Retailer (4.7%).
$9.7M API (auto-orders, not buyer-initiated) brings total digital to 17.8%.
Even the sell side that goes online is mostly API (machine-to-machine), not eCommerce (buyer-initiated).
Implication: Fix the LIST step to unlock the SELL step. The product already enters digitally (BUY works). But if wholesalers don't list their catalog online, retailers have nothing to buy. Catalog coverage initiatives (Initiative 2) attack this directly.

Why It Jams at Wholesaler → Retailer

Four structural barriers keeping $73.8M offline

Structural Barriers

1
CATALOG: Products not listed online. Self-fulfilling prophecy confirmed. BDC has 22,102 products, only 16.4% online. If it's not listed, it can't be sold. This is the root cause.
2
STANDING ORDERS: $39.3M wholesaler SOs offline. No digital alternative. Retailers place recurring orders by phone/fax. No product exists to convert these to online. (Total SOs across all layers: $142.5M offline.)
3
TRUST: Holidays hurt online %. When stakes are high (Valentine's, Mother's Day), buyers revert to offline. They don't trust the platform for critical orders. Online % drops precisely when volume peaks.
4
CONCENTRATION: Remove Mayesh + KBC = $16.2M digital total. The digital wholesaler base is thin. Two accounts drive the majority. If either stops, the layer collapses.
Order of attack: (1) Fix catalog coverage to unlock potential → (2) build digital SO product to capture recurring volume → (3) earn trust through holiday reliability → (4) expand beyond Mayesh/KBC to diversify.
