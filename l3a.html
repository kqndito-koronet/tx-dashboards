L3a · What Do the Big 3 Wholesalers Do Differently?

Mayesh, KBC, and Bill Doran are the only wholesalers with meaningful online. This dashboard compares their models to understand what works and what to replicate.
Three completely different digital models — each with lessons.
  • Bill Doran = eCommerce growth story. ~200 external eCom buyers (after filtering inter-branch transfers) but +67% growth, +77% more buyers, fees +321%. Every month accelerating. The one to watch.
  • KBC = broadest base (~1,500 eCom + 564 API buyers) but sells 90% Boxes online while 89% of offline is Units. The bunch upload gap is massive.
  • Mayesh = API model (24% online share) but declining everywhere: offline -24%, API -14%, buyers -23%. Business health issue, not channel issue.
  • The product gap is NOT about what products — roses/hydrangeas sell both online and offline. The gap is inventory format (Units not listed) and buyer behavior (phone habit).

What to replicate
Bill Doran's model: list ALL inventory online (Units + Boxes), grow buyers one by one (+77% YoY). Small base but accelerating every month. Simple eCommerce, no API needed.
KBC's base: 175 online buyers is the broadest of any wholesaler. But they need to list their Units ($49M offline not available online).
Mayesh's cautionary tale: high online share doesn't mean healthy. They're losing buyers across all channels.

Mayesh's digital model: API, not eCommerce

Mayesh's "online" is API-based ($22.7M) — system-to-system integration, not buyer browsing an eShop. Their API buyers are high-value ($112K/buyer vs $73K offline). But the API is also declining.

Top API products: Rose $3.4M, Dutch Mixed Box $1.5M, Hydrangea $1.3M, Garden Rose $820K, Lisianthus $752K.
Inventory: 71% Boxes via API, 64% Boxes offline. Mayesh is a box-oriented business.
Lesson
Mayesh proves that high online share ≠ healthy. Their 24% online share is actually declining in absolute dollars. The lesson for other wholesalers: focus on growing the buyer base and total GMV, not just online share. Bill Doran's 3.5% growing fast is healthier than Mayesh's 24% shrinking.

Replication playbook
Step 1: List Units/loose stems on eShop (KBC's gap — $49M offline not available).
Step 2: Same products, same prices — no need for a different catalog. Bill Doran proves it works.
Step 3: Hard goods (glass, foam, supplies) stay offline for now — phone reorder habit, not urgent.
Step 4: Focus on core florals first (roses, hydrangeas, carnations, greens) — they drive 80% of eCom GMV.

The adoption curve
KBC has ~21% of buyers online (~1,500 eCom customer_names out of 7,053 offline). If the remaining ~79% came online, that's ~5,500 buyers × $63K avg eCom GMV/buyer = massive upside. But first they need to list Units.
